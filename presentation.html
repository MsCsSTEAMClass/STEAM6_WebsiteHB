Project Presentation

Your team has designed a solution to an environmental problem. Now it's time to convince your audience that your solution is worth using.

Your presentation should explain the problem, your solution, how your system works, what you learned through testing, and why your solution matters.

Include the following information in your presentation:

1. The Problem

Your audience needs to understand the problem before they can understand your solution.

Include:

  • What environmental issue are you addressing?
  • What causes it?
  • Who or what is affected?
  • Why does it matter?
  • What evidence did you find during your research?

Goal: Make us care about the problem.


3. How Your System Works

This is the technical heart of the presentation.

INPUT → PROCESS → OUTPUT

Explain:

  • What is your sensor / input?
  • What variable stores your sensor data?
  • What threshold or range did you choose?
  • What happens when the threshold / range is reached?
  • What outputs respond?
  • How do your components interact?
  • What role do the LEDs play?
  • What role does the moving component play?
  • What does the micro:bit display communicate?
  • What does the button do?

Goal: Make us care about the problem.




5. Why Should We Use It?

Make this its own slide(s) or section, rather than letting it get buried in your conclusion.

Answer:

  • Why is your solution useful?
  • What problem does it help address?
  • What are the environmental benefits?
  • What makes your solution effective?
  • Who could use a system like this?
  • What would happen if your solution were implemented?

Then:

Convince us. Why should someone choose your solution?


2. Your Proposed Solution

Now introduce your idea.

Include:

  • What solution did your team design?
  • How does your solution address the environmental problem?
  • What does your physical model represent?
  • How could this solution help?

Goal: Explain what you designed and why.





4. Test & Iteration

This is where you'll discuss your engineering process.

Answer:

  • What did you test?
  • What worked?
  • What didn't work?
  • What problem did you encounter?
  • How did you debug it?
  • What did you change?
  • WHY did you make that change?
  • How did the change improve your system?

Include at least one before & after example

Example:

Problem: Our sensor triggered too easily.
Change: We adjusted the threshold.
Result: The system now responds only when conditions reach the intended range.



6. Future Improvements

Before you end your presentation, answer at least two of the following:

  • What would you change if you had more time?
  • What would you improve with better materials, additional sensors, or more testing?
  • What would your next version look like?

Presentation Checklist

Before presenting, make sure your presentation includes:

  • Environmental problem & evidence
  • Proposed solution
  • How the system works
  • Input, variable, logic, threshold, and outputs
  • Testing & debugging
  • At least one design improvement
  • Environmental impact
  • Why your solution should be used
  • Future improvements
  • All team members participate
