Research


What do I need to learn about my environmental problem before I can design a solution?


Your job as an engineer

Before you can design a solution, you need to understand the problem.

Your team will research your chosen environmental issue to determine what is happening, why it is happening, and how your Hummingbird system could model or respond to it.

Your research should help you answer these 5 questions:


1. What is the environmental problem?

Identify & explain your issue.

  • What is happening?
  • Where does it happen?
  • Who or what is affected?
  • Why is it important?

Be specific! "Pollution" is a broad topic. "Air pollution caused by vehicle emissions in urban areas" gives you something you can actually investigate.




3. What data could help us understand it?

Your Hummingbird system will use a sensor to collect information from its environment. Your research should help you determine what that sensor measures and why that measurement matters.

Think about:

  • What are we measuring?
  • What does the measurement tell us?
  • What values would be considered
    normal, safe, dangerous, etc.?
  • Is there a useful range or threshold?

Your research guide already identifies temperature, light, sound, and motion as possible inputs and emphasizes explaining what the collected data and chosen range mean.

2. What causes the problem?

Look for cause-and-effect relationships.

Ask:
What changes in the environment?
↓
What causes the change?
↓
What happens as a result?

Your final system should model a real relationship, not simply turning something on because a sensor detects a value. Your project guide specifically requires students to demonstrate a real-world cause-and-effect relationship.



4. What happens when conditions change?

This is where your research starts becoming engineering design.

Complete this idea:

When _____ changes, _____ happens because _____.

Then consider:

If our sensor detects this change, how could our system respond?

For example:

If the temperature rises above a safe range, a warning system could activate.

The important part is the why. Your system's response should make sense based on what you learned about the environmental problem.


5. What solutions already exist?

You don't have to invent something that has never existed before.
Research how people currently address your environmental issue.

Ask:

  • What solutions are already being used?
  • What technology is involved?
  • What works well?
  • What limitations or challenges exist?
  • Could your system model, improve, or demonstrate part of an existing solution?



Your Research Checklist

Before your move on, your team should be able to:

  • Clearly explain your environmental issue
  • Explain at least one real cause-and-effect relationship
  • Identify what environmental data is relevant
  • Explain what your sensor will measure
  • Identify a useful range or threshold
  • Explain why your system's response makes sense
  • Describe how your prototype could help address the issue
  • Record the sources you used
